- Immigrating from bangladesh
- I moved to the US from Bangladesh with my family when I was eight. My first years in NYC were filled with a lot of firsts: eating New York pizza, touching snow, watching Home Alone 2, and rollerskating in the summer. I was pretty shy in a foreign country, so I joined storytelling contests and ran in class elections to get better at public speaking.
- GROWING UP IN QUEENS, NYC
- My mother raised two daughters on her own in NYC. Around the same time, I was diagnosed with deafness in my right ear. A lot of my childhood involved navigating doctors, insurance, translation, and trying to make sense of systems that weren’t built with us in mind. I also started working under the table at 12 to help support my family.
- Growing up in Queens, NYC
- Falling for science
- FALLING FOR SCIENCE
- I studied biomedicine at Brooklyn Tech High School. I was lucky to have teachers who really supported my interest in science and engineering. My favorite unexpected classes were wood shop and an after-school class where we read scientific journal articles to make sense of applied research.
- COLLEGE YEARS & STUDYING ABROAD
- I studied Biomechanical Engineering at Syracuse University and spent a year abroad at City University of London. I was President of the National Society of Black Engineers and a leader in our school mentorship program supporting women of color. Working as a barista in London, traveling all over Europe and Egypt, working in bioinstrumentaion labs and getting my first tattoo were peak college years.
- College years & studying abroad
- SCIENTIFIC RESEARCH
- LAB RESEARCH
- I worked in research labs through college and med school across bioengineering, pharmacology, orthopedic surgery, thoracic surgery, and cancer research, including NSF programs, SUNY Upstate, and Roswell Park Cancer Institute. Science and engineering trained me to ask better questions, be comfortable with unknowns, look for evidence, and keep testing my assumptions.
- PIVOTING & REINVENTING AGAIN
- I left my medical career path and moved into startups. It was a lean-budget, zero-certainty phase of life: a mattress on my mom’s basement floor, startup operations, SaaS product strategy, and a lot of figuring things out in real time. My work also took me to London, Tokyo, and São Paulo to facilitate workshops for entrepreneurs, and I took a ton of courses while building a new foundation for myself.
- STARTUP ADVENTURES
- AD COUNCIL
- PUBLIC INTEREST AND INNOVATION
- I joined the Ad Council to work on products and stayed for nine years, where I started and led the Innovation Practice. My work expanded across audience research, growth and adoption strategy, executive advisory councils, emerging technology, and helping teams adopt new ways of working. I got to work on efforts like the Emmy Award-winning Love Has No Labels and the $52M COVID vaccine education initiative, where product, trust, behavior, and public impact were all deeply connected.
- LEADING TRANSFORMATION AT ANOMALY
- At Anomaly, my work moved further into customer experience, commercial strategy, AI adoption, and product direction. I led CX and GTM transformation work for clients, helping teams make sense of customer behavior, emerging technology, and what was actually worth building.It was a chapter where strategy, customer value, and commercial value had to work much more closely together.
- ANOMALY
- THINKING IN PUBLIC
- USING MY PUBLIC VOICE TO INVITE CONVERSATIONS
- Over the years, I’ve given more than 200 talks, panels, workshops, and executive sessions, including at the United Nations, Google, TikTok, and Columbia University. Public speaking has become one of the ways I think in public: testing ideas, challenging assumptions, and making sense of how technology, business, media, and human behavior are changing at the same time.
- BUILDING UNMISSABLES
- I started Unmissables, a podcast and newsletter, because I wanted better conversations about technology, business, human behavior, and trust than the ones I was finding. It’s also where I’ve been developing ideas like Cognitive Endurance, my framework for thinking about judgment, agency, and what it means to keep your thinking your own in an AI-shaped world.
- Building Unmissables
- now
- RIGHT NOW
- Right now, my work spans strategy, AI, customer experience, writing, and speaking. That includes advisory work, CX sprints, Unmissables, and collaborations with teams thinking through how technology and changing customer expectations should shape what they build, how they grow, and how they stay useful. Outside of work, I’m happiest being a mom to my son, a partner to my husband and dabbling in creative quests.

- The Questions We're Exploring
- There are bigger questions about AI than what it can do.
- AI is becoming part of how we work, learn, create, make decisions, and relate to one another. That leaves us with questions that aren't answered by knowing what the technology can do.
- Where do we want AI to be, and what do we still want to experience ourselves?
- •
- What happens to relationships when AI becomes something we talk to, confide in, learn from, or depend on?
- •
- What social norms will emerge for a generation growing up with AI as an ordinary part of childhood?
- •
- What happens to education when answers are always available? What should learning be for then?
- •
- What happens to creativity and authorship when making becomes easier?
- •
- Keeping up isn't the same as knowing what you think.
- So much of what we see about AI has already been interpreted for us: bookmarked, half-read, meant to revisit. And even when we do sit with it, it's just us and our own head. Some of these questions never come up at work. Social media isn't built for an unfinished thought; it wants your final take, not your open question.
- What if we made time for the things worth understanding?
- You don't have to find the good stuff. That's my job. Each month I bring together a small set of material that looks at one question from a few different angles. Then we make sense of it together: not to land on a conclusion, but to become better participants - better citizens - in a world AI is already changing.
